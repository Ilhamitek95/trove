## Where Trove works: website and social

Trove now covers Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah. Sharjah is in because many makers and providers work there, where rent
is cheaper, and its art and design colleges are full of student makers. The website and the posts say "Dubai and Abu
Dhabi" throughout, so update both together.

Two separate decisions:
1. **Where makers and providers can be based.** Limited by where the courier collects and how far providers travel.
2. **Where buyers can receive deliveries.** Where the delivery and liability risks sit: returns, damage in transit,
   delivery cost.

The first can be wider than the second. A maker in Sharjah can sell to buyers in Dubai and Abu Dhabi.

### The other emirates

| Area | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ajman | Add it now, alongside Sharjah | Joins straight onto Sharjah, rents as low or lower, courier collection should cost about the same. |
| Umm Al Quwain, Ras Al Khaimah, Fujairah | Don't advertise them, but let makers and providers there apply | Honest message: "We're starting in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah. Elsewhere in the UAE? Apply anyway, and we'll tell you when we reach you." Builds a list of real makers by area. |
| Al Ain | Decide whether "Abu Dhabi" means the city or the whole emirate | Al Ain is in Abu Dhabi emirate, so people there will assume they're included. Check what the courier covers. |

### Opening more areas later
- Set a trigger for each area, e.g. 10 strong applicants and an acceptable courier quote.
- Keep courier costs down: collect weekly from the further emirates rather than per sale, or let makers there drop off at one point in Sharjah.
- Providers are the easier case. Nothing is shipped; the provider is responsible for the service. A provider in Ras Al Khaimah who will travel to Dubai can list now, as long as the Provider Agreement says they cover their own travel.

### Student makers
- Visa rules: students on a student visa may need a permit to earn money, even when Trove is the one buying their pieces. Add to the business adviser's questions, next to "no trade licence to start".
- Age: makers and providers must be 18 or over. Check the Seller and Provider Agreements say so.
- Capacity: students go quiet at exam time and many leave after graduating. Make pausing a shop easy; use the "orders you could handle monthly" answer.
- Reaching them: partner with the art and design colleges (American University of Sharjah's College of Architecture, Art and Design; University of Sharjah's College of Fine Arts and Design). Degree shows and student societies are good places to start.
- A "student makers" spotlight fits the campaign. Keep the terms the same as for every other maker.

### Inside each carousel
Every swipe should look different from the last. Five rules:
- No two slides in a row share a background.
- Alternate photo and colour card: photo, card, photo, card. Photos carry colour through props (yarn, flowers, brass, glass). Cards carry it through the background.
- Build each carousel from one colour family, led by the post's lead colour:
  - Warm: Clay, Pink, Tan, Cream. Keep Pink away from Sage.
  - Cool: Light Blue, Light Green, Sage, Cream. Keep Light Blue away from Orange.
  - Seasonal: Pastel Orange (in photos only), Orange accents, Tan, Cream.
- Give the key slide the most contrast. The slide with the offer, the 60% or "apply" goes on Charcoal with Cream text, or Cream with one word in Orange.
- Keep the end card the same every time: Cream, the wordmark and the apply link.

Example "Make things by hand? You're a maker." (six slides, warm): 1 cover photo with Pink props · 2 Pink card, Charcoal
text · 3 photo, Clay and Tan · 4 example shop page on Cream · 5 the offer, Charcoal card, Cream text · 6 end card, Cream.

Example "What Trove costs, and what you keep." (eight slides, cool): 1 cover Sage card · 2–7 one question each, rotate
Cream, Light Blue, photo, Light Green, Cream, photo; the "you keep 60%" slide goes on Charcoal · 8 end card, Cream.

For the name-tag scenes: let the pieces set the colour. Keep Trove's overlays and end card in the brand palette.
Vary the textures: metal and beads, yarn, canvas and paint, glass, paper and clay, not only linen and wood. Vary the
settings: kitchen table, sofa, window, balcony, market stall.
Limits: no more than two accent colours in one frame, nothing neon or saturated, text always Charcoal or Cream with
enough contrast.

## Show every kind of maker

The brand look belongs to Trove's own design: the type, the cards, the overlays, the end card. It shouldn't decide what
the makers' work looks like. If every piece shown is a neutral ceramic or oatmeal linen, a maker whose work is bright,
patterned or traditional will assume Trove isn't for them. Keep Trove's frame consistent; let the pieces inside vary.

Vary across the set: style (minimal, colourful, patterned, traditional, Arabic calligraphy, playful pieces for
children, modern and graphic); colour (bold where the maker's work is bold: cobalt glazes, jewel-toned beads, bright
yarn; the brand palette applies to Trove's cards and type, not the pieces); craft (ceramics, textiles, jewellery,
prints and art, stationery, candles, children's and pet pieces, plus the services); price (small gifts alongside
statement pieces); people (hands of different ages, skin tones and genders).

Simple check: in any carousel or Reel showing several pieces, at least one should sit clearly outside the neutral palette.

For the post 2 Reel, mix the styles: Earrings classic (pearl or fine gold, for a bride) · Blanket playful (soft pastel
stripes) · Cushion bold (geometric or traditional pattern in terracotta and indigo) · Painting colourful and abstract ·
Chair carved wood, re-covered in a bold print.

For the generated images: write variety into the prompts ("bright", "patterned", "jewel-toned") so the AI doesn't
default to beige.

Use the line "Any style. Made by hand." in three places: post 3 "Makers. Providers. Buyers." (a slide straight after the
Makers slide, over a grid of four very different pieces); post 4 "Make things by hand? You're a maker." (opening line
of the caption); post 12 "From your hands to your own shop." (the last on-screen line before the end card).
